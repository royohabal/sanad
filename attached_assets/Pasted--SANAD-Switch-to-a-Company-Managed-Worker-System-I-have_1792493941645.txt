# SANAD — Switch to a Company-Managed Worker System

I have changed SANAD's business model. **There must be no independent workers. All workers must belong to a registered company that manages their SANAD accounts.**

Inspect the existing codebase, MongoDB models, Clerk authentication, worker accounts, QR codes, wallets, tips, settlements, and payment implementation before making changes. Modify the existing project instead of rebuilding it.

Preserve the existing parent/child wallet features and individual send/request functionality. This change applies to the worker and company system.

## 1. Company registration and dashboard

- Companies register and create their own company account.
- Each company has a secure company-admin dashboard.
- A company administrator can add, edit, deactivate, and manage workers belonging to that company.
- The company dashboard displays the company's worker list, individual worker tip totals, aggregate tip reports, and monthly settlement information.
- Companies must only be able to access workers and records belonging to their own company.
- Preserve the existing company approval or onboarding process if one exists.

## 2. Company creates worker accounts

- Remove the independent-worker registration and onboarding flow.
- Companies create worker profiles and worker login accounts from the company dashboard.
- Each worker must have a unique account linked to exactly one company at a time.
- Store the worker's company association in MongoDB and enforce it on the server.
- Use the existing Clerk authentication system. Follow Clerk's supported account-creation and invitation approach rather than storing passwords in MongoDB or exposing administrative credentials.
- Give workers a secure way to receive their invitation, activate their account, sign in, and recover access.
- Prevent duplicate worker accounts and duplicate email assignments where inappropriate.
- Company administrators may manage their own workers but must not be able to access another company's records.
- If the existing app has a platform-admin role, preserve its separation from company administrators.

## 3. Worker dashboard

Every worker must be able to sign in to their own SANAD account and see only their own permitted information.

Create or update the worker dashboard to include:
- Worker name and profile
- Company name
- Personal worker QR code
- A button to download the QR code as a printable image
- A print-friendly QR code page with the worker's name and SANAD branding
- Total tips received
- Current tip balance
- Transaction history with dates, amounts in KWD, and statuses
- Monthly tip summary, if supported by the existing data
- Clear loading, empty, and error states

Workers can view their own wallet and tip history, but cannot edit their balance, create fake tips, change their company, or view another worker's private data.

## 4. QR codes and public tipping

- Each company-managed worker receives a unique, stable QR code.
- Scanning the QR code opens that worker's public tipping page.
- Public customers must be able to tip without registering for a SANAD account.
- Display the correct worker and, where appropriate, the company name.
- A public QR code must not expose the worker's email, Clerk user ID, private wallet information, or internal database IDs.
- Do not change an existing worker's QR identifier unnecessarily.
- When a worker is deactivated or leaves a company, prevent unauthorized future account access and apply a clear policy to the public QR page. Preserve historical tips and transaction records.
- Prevent one worker's QR code from being used to credit another worker's wallet.

## 5. Worker wallets and tips

- Every worker has a company-linked digital tip wallet or ledger.
- Record tips in MongoDB and associate each tip with the correct worker and company.
- Workers can view their current balance, total tips received, and transaction history.
- The server calculates balances from authoritative ledger records or a correctly maintained balance, not from values submitted by the browser.
- Preserve existing tip records, ratings, checkout records, worker ledgers, and settlement records.
- Do not erase old records when changing the worker model.
- Ensure every new tip is recorded once and cannot be duplicated through repeated requests or payment callbacks.

## 6. Company settlements

- Preserve the existing monthly settlement feature.
- Companies can review monthly tip totals for each worker.
- Show salary and tips separately wherever salary information exists.
- Prevent the same tips from being included in multiple settlements.
- Maintain an auditable history of settlement periods and statuses.
- Do not claim that a settlement or payout has reached a bank account unless an actual supported payout integration exists. For this university project, keep settlement actions clearly labelled as simulated if no real payout provider is configured.

## 7. Payment integration

- Preserve the existing worker tip checkout, parent child-wallet top-up, individual transfers, and money requests.
- Continue investigating the Stripe configuration separately. Do not let a Stripe failure break worker account creation, QR generation, or wallet history.
- Use Stripe test Checkout only if the configured test account successfully creates a valid Checkout Session in the chosen currency.
- Otherwise, keep the clearly labelled simulated KWD checkout for the university demo.
- Do not silently convert KWD to another currency.
- Never credit a worker's wallet because a success URL was opened. Verify payments server-side or record an explicitly simulated demo payment.
- Internal individual-to-individual transfers must continue to use the internal wallet ledger, not an external checkout for every transfer.

## 8. Security and migration

- Preserve MongoDB and Clerk.
- Remove or disable independent-worker registration, worker self-onboarding, and any independent-worker-only withdrawal flow.
- Inspect old independent-worker records before deciding how to handle them. Do not delete records, tips, or ledgers. Report whether existing workers need to be assigned to a company.
- Protect all private routes and APIs with server-side authentication and authorization.
- Ensure company administrators can access only their own company and its workers.
- Ensure workers can access only their own worker profile, QR code, wallet, and tip history.
- Apply rate limiting to public tipping endpoints and validate all amounts on the server.
- Preserve parent/child wallet permissions and individual transfer authorization.
- Do not expose secrets, private account information, or wallet data in public endpoints.
- Do not modify production data or deploy automatically.

## 9. Testing and final report

Run relevant typechecks, tests, and production builds.

Verify:
1. A company can register or access its authorized company dashboard.
2. A company can create a worker and provide secure login access.
3. The worker can sign in and view their own dashboard.
4. The worker can download and print their own QR code.
5. Scanning the QR code opens the correct worker's tipping page.
6. A tip is recorded against the correct worker and company.
7. A worker cannot access another worker's private information.
8. One company cannot access another company's workers or settlements.
9. Old tips and ledgers remain intact.
10. Parent/child wallets, individual transfers, money requests, and existing payment flows still work.
11. Independent-worker registration and withdrawal are no longer offered.
12. No duplicate tips or settlements can be created.

At the end, provide a clear report of changed files, routes, database changes, migration requirements, tests actually passed, remaining limitations, and the deployment steps needed for both frontend and API.

Do not claim any test passed unless it was actually executed. Do not deploy or delete production data.
